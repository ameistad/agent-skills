# Rewrite Patterns

Use these patterns selectively. They are prompts for judgment, not a banned-word list. Keep formal or technical language when it is accurate and natural for the audience.

## Start near the point

Remove openings that delay the actual message.

- "I hope this message finds you well. I am writing to inform you that..." can often start with the information itself.
- "In today's rapidly evolving landscape..." usually adds no context. Replace it with the specific change or problem.
- Keep a greeting or brief social opening when the relationship or channel calls for one.

## Cut filler and empty emphasis

Delete phrases that announce importance without explaining it, such as "it is worth noting that" or "it is important to mention." Replace intensifiers such as "truly" or "incredibly" with evidence, or remove them.

Do not remove emphasis that conveys real degree, emotion, urgency, or risk.

## Prefer actions over abstractions

Turn noun-heavy phrases into direct verbs when meaning stays intact.

| Stiff | More direct |
|---|---|
| make an assessment of | assess |
| provide an explanation of | explain |
| perform an analysis of | analyze |
| facilitate collaboration | help the teams work together |
| achieve an improvement in | improve |

Keep the longer form when the noun names a formal artifact, process, or domain concept.

## Simplify vocabulary in context

Prefer the familiar word when it carries the same meaning:

- use instead of utilize
- help instead of facilitate
- start instead of commence
- before instead of prior to
- to instead of in order to
- about instead of approximately in conversational text

Do not replace a precise term merely because it sounds formal. One occurrence is rarely a problem; clusters of elevated defaults are the stronger signal.

## Preserve meaningful uncertainty

Remove social hedges that only soften a clear request: "I just wanted to ask if perhaps you could..."

Keep epistemic, legal, scientific, and safety qualifiers. `May`, `can`, `must`, `likely`, and similar words are not interchangeable. A rewrite must not change possibility into certainty or advice into a requirement.

## Vary rhythm without performing imperfection

- Split a long sentence when its clauses compete for attention.
- Combine consecutive short sentences when the result sounds choppy.
- Let an important short sentence stand on its own.
- Avoid giving every paragraph the same shape.
- Do not add slang, fragments, typos, or random asides solely to appear human.

## Make structure earn its place

Use prose for a single flowing argument. Use bullets for genuinely parallel items, steps, choices, or scan-friendly facts. Do not force a set of three, add decorative headings, or bold arbitrary phrases.

## Clarify procedural writing

For instructions where order matters:

- Put a condition before the action it controls: "If the status is `failed`, run `app repair`."
- Give one action per step when combined actions could be missed or performed out of order.
- Keep commands, identifiers, paths, numbers, and UI labels exact.
- Repeat a technical name when a synonym could create ambiguity.

These constraints serve accurate execution, not every kind of prose.

## Final integrity pass

Compare the rewrite with the source and confirm:

- Every claim, number, name, and requirement still means the same thing.
- No quotation, command, path, identifier, error message, or UI label changed accidentally.
- No example, opinion, or personal detail was invented.
- The rewrite sounds appropriate for its language, audience, and channel.
- Each remaining sentence contributes something distinct.
